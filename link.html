about me

Artificial Intelligence, commonly known as AI, is one of the most important and rapidly developing technologies in the modern world. It is a branch of computer science that focuses on developing machines, computer systems, and software capable of performing tasks that usually require human intelligence. These tasks include learning, reasoning, problem-solving, decision-making, understanding languages, recognizing images, and identifying patterns. Artificial Intelligence enables computers to analyze large amounts of information, learn from experience, and provide useful results with limited human intervention. Today, AI has become an essential part of everyday life, influencing education, healthcare, business, transportation, agriculture, entertainment, communication, and many other fields. Examples of AI applications include virtual assistants, chatbots, recommendation systems, facial recognition, self-driving vehicles, language translation tools, and intelligent robots. Many people use AI without even realizing it because it is integrated into smartphones, search engines, social media platforms, online shopping websites, and digital payment systems. As technology continues to advance, Artificial Intelligence is expected to play an even greater role in shaping the future of society. The concept of Artificial Intelligence originated from the idea of creating machines that could imitate human thinking and behavior. Although the idea of intelligent machines existed in stories and philosophical discussions for centuries, AI became a formal area of scientific research during the twentieth century. In 1950, British mathematician Alan Turing introduced an important discussion about whether machines could think. He proposed a method of evaluating a machine's ability to demonstrate intelligent behavior, which later became known as the Turing Test. In 1956, the term Artificial Intelligence became widely associated with a research workshop organized at Dartmouth College in the United States. Researchers began exploring ways to make computers solve mathematical problems, play games, understand language, and perform logical reasoning. Early AI systems were limited because computers had relatively low processing power, storage capacity was expensive, and large amounts of digital data were not readily available.
